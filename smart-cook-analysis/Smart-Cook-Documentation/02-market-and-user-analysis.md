# 02 — Market and User Analysis

## 1. Market framing

Smart Cook operates at the intersection of:
- recipe discovery;
- ingredient and pantry management;
- meal planning and shopping;
- guided cooking;
- user-generated culinary content;
- AI-assisted cooking.

The product should compete on the integrated workflow, not on recipe count alone.

## 2. Competitive landscape

Earlier research identified products and categories including:
- SuperCook: ingredient-based recipe discovery.
- Cooklist: pantry, shopping, and recipe organization.
- Samsung Food / Whisk: recipe and food-planning ecosystem.
- Cookpad: recipe community and user-generated content.
- ReciMe: recipe saving and organization.
- KitchenPal and similar products: pantry management.
- Arabic recipe publishers and cooking apps.

These references are research leads, not a current verified feature/pricing matrix. Revalidate live product status, regional availability, pricing, and capabilities before investor or launch materials are prepared.

## 3. Opportunity hypotheses

1. A global platform can personalize by language and cuisine while keeping discovery open.
2. Structured recipes connected to pantry, shopping, and cook mode create more repeat utility than passive browsing.
3. Creator publishing can build differentiated content if quality, rights, and moderation are handled credibly.
4. AI can reduce recipe-entry and translation friction, but must not become the authority for safety or publication.

These are hypotheses to test, not proven market facts.

## 4. Personas

### Persona A — Culinary explorer
- Wants to discover unfamiliar cuisines.
- Values photos, clear ingredients, cultural context, and reliable steps.
- Needs language and unit localization.

### Persona B — Beginner cook
- Needs simple explanations, timers, serving guidance, and confidence.
- Benefits from clear difficulty and preparation-time estimates.
- Needs visible allergen and food-safety warnings.

### Persona C — Household kitchen manager
- Tracks available ingredients and upcoming expiry.
- Wants meal plans and consolidated shopping lists.
- Values quick suggestions and household-size scaling.

### Persona D — Recipe creator
- Wants to document recipes and build a recognizable profile.
- Needs a transparent review process and clear attribution.
- Must understand the exclusive license and withdrawal process before submitting.

### Persona E — Reviewer / moderator
- Needs a queue, structured checklists, clear escalation rules, and audit history.
- Must not approve their own content.
- Needs tools to explain decisions and request changes.

## 5. Core user journeys

### Discovery-to-cooking
Open app → browse personalized/global feed → inspect recipe → adjust servings → add missing items to shopping list → start cook mode → complete cooking session → optionally rate outcome.

### Pantry-to-recipe
Add ingredients → normalize ingredient names → match recipes → filter by available ingredients, time, diet, and cuisine → choose recipe → cook.

### Creator submission
Create draft → map ingredients and steps → attach media → complete rights declaration → submit → automated checks → human review → approved/private or published, or needs changes/rejected → creator receives status and reason.

### Translation
Open source recipe → select target language → display original and translation → mark machine-generated translation → allow qualified correction → preserve translation version history and attribution.

## 6. Research questions to validate

- Which first-use action produces the strongest retention: pantry entry, discovery, or recipe creation?
- How many users are willing to submit recipes under an exclusive commercial license without direct payment?
- Which creator benefits materially improve acceptance?
- What review turnaround time is operationally sustainable?
- Which cuisines and languages should receive editorial investment first?
- What proportion of recipes require specialist safety or rights review?

## 7. Validation methods

- Moderated usability tests across different languages and cooking experience levels.
- Creator interviews and agreement comprehension tests.
- Prototype tests for recipe creation and ingredient mapping.
- Small invited creator pilot before public community launch.
- Cohort analysis of first cooking session and repeat cooking.
- Review-queue simulation using realistic recipe submissions.
